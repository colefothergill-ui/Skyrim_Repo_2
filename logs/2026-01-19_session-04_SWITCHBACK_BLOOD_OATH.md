# Session 04 - SWITCHBACK BLOOD OATH
Date: 2026-01-19
Act: 2
Scene: S4_SWITCHBACK_AMBUSH
Hold: The Reach
Location: The Reach - Switchback approach to Markarth (Crescent Witness ambush)

The war does not end when the horns fall silent.

Whiterun held - stone and smoke and stubborn breath - yet victory arrived like a blade that kept cutting after it left the hand. By day, the Jarl's hall swelled with praise and procedure, and Agran Moorcroft stood among it all as if the world's noise could not shake his charm loose. He wore blood beneath the armor like a private hymn. He accepted the Brevet - acting authority, battlefield permission - without demanding parchment or pomp. The Empire's bureaucracy would catch up later. The deeds had already signed him.

But there are victories that draw eyes.

Rumors spread along the wall like fire licking dry grass: a red-haired duelist who met Galmar Stone-Fist and did not die. A bard who fought like a surgeon. A man whose gaze made even Proventus Avanici stammer. Some called it courage. Some called it witchwork. Those who spoke of golden eyes spoke more quietly, and only when they thought no one listened.

Then the world blinked.

Midnight on the road came like a misremembered sentence. A seam split. A breath held too long. Agran found himself displaced - no tavern light, no city gate, no Hadvar at his shoulder - only the Reach's hard silence and the long, cold geometry of stone. In one continuity, the ring was presented. In another, it was already on his hand. Only Agran seemed to feel both truths at once, as if reality itself had tried to correct the story with a hurried, bloodstained edit.

Hadvar was missing - then found - then nearly stolen away from life.

The courier line to Solitude became a trap laid with intent rather than chance. A watcher in the rocks. A dose of poison meant for Hadvar's veins. A crude attempt to rupture the chain-of-custody: to test the seal, to test the man carrying it, to see what he would do when pressed between honor and hunger. Hadvar's body betrayed him first - breath shallow, pulse wrong. Agran's fury answered second.

He did not plead with the night.

He made it obey.

Along the switchback approach to Markarth, the Crescent Witness cell revealed itself - crossbows slotted into high ground, angles chosen to pin and herd. They did not open with a single shot. They opened with doctrine: suppression, control, containment. Their leader barked formal commands as if the words themselves were chains. "Drop the sealed evidence. Do not run. This is a test."

Agran ran anyway, but not like a man.

Bolts struck stone; one bit into shoulder and blood went hot then cold. He vanished between pillars, reappeared where the eye could not track, and turned their clean ambush into a broken, stuttering mess. The Reach became a diagram, and Agran became the hand moving pieces. Sightlines shattered. Their kill-zone inverted. The moment they scrambled to reload, he was already inside them.

What followed will not travel politely in official reports. It will go by rumor first: a scream that did not end cleanly, a red smear on pale rock, and the sound of a crossbow falling useless to the ground. Men trained to observe and withdraw found themselves trapped in a story that had changed genres. Crossbows became clubs. Knives became prayers. Discipline became panic.

Agran fed.

Not once. Not twice. Enough that the night itself seemed to lean closer to watch.

By the time the last runner tried to flee, Agran's hand was already on his shoulder and steel was already through him. The body fell with a dull certainty. Above, the field leader - handler, doctrine-man, the one meant to return to the shadows with a neat little account - stared down as if his faith in procedure had finally met something older than paperwork.

Agran's leap into the slot was not a climb. It was a declaration: that gravity was negotiable when rage and strength agreed.

The leader reached for a whistle cord to call the next layer of the trap. Agran caught the wrist mid-motion. The man did not get to finish the first syllable of a lie. Fangs found vein; blood sprayed across crag. The scream cut short under the force of a feeding that was not indulgence so much as judgment. When it ended, the leader's fingers slackened and the cord hung useless, swinging like a failed escape plan.

The cell died on that stone.

But the story did not.

Because the dead carried paper.

Among the bodies: a dispatch tube, a seal kit, a slate-tag marked with the crescent-over-tower sigil, and an instruction slip torn as if someone had carried it too long and too nervously. The words were spare - coldly professional.

"SUBJECT CONFIRMED."
"TEST SEAL COMPLETE."
"REPORT TO: MARKARTH DROP."
Signed only: "--THE WITNESS."

No name. No face. A title that felt like a hand on the back of Agran's neck.

The blood did its own remembering. With the leader's life came a new echo - ink-sting on the tongue, a whistle cord, a door that shouldn't be there, and a low voice shaping a command: "Bring him to stone." Whether that "stone" is Markarth's walls or something deeper beneath them remains uncertain. The Reach has caverns enough for gods to hide in, and Markarth has always loved keeping secrets in its ribs.

Now Agran stands amid the aftermath: cloak torn, armor spotted, mouth stained, eyes still too bright. Hadvar - shield-brother, courier, friend - remains stabilized off-screen at the Imperial checkpoint, vulnerable to the next attempt unless guarded and moved with care. The sealed evidence remains intact, but it has been tested and marked; someone wanted to see whether Agran would break, and in answering, he revealed exactly what the watchers came to confirm.

If Markarth is Imperial-safe, it is also Daedric-close. The city is stone, and stone remembers. And somewhere within that carved throat of a city, a "drop" waits - along with whatever thinks it has the right to watch, to test, to claim.

Tonight, the Reach has learned a simpler truth:

Do not hunt the Bloody Bard in the dark unless you are prepared to be the chorus.
