# Session 06 - CITRUS RECKONING: RAGS, WOLVES, AND COLDHARBOUR
Date: 2026-01-23
Act: 2
Scene: S9_MOURNFUL_THRONE_FALLOUT → S16_REACH_RIDGELINE_KILLBOX (→ S17_CITRUS_RECKONING_AFTERMATH)
Hold: The Reach
Location: Markarth (Understone Keep / Mournful Throne / Temple of Dibella) → Cidhna Mine → The Reach (ridgeline killbox)

Markarth does not celebrate. Markarth tallies.

When Agran Moorcroft returned from the undercity with blood on his cloak and paper in his satchel, the city did what it always does when truth arrives: it tried to turn truth into procedure. Proof was weighed. Names were recited. Seals were inspected like holy relics. And there, under the Mournful Throne, the Wolf of Solitude made corruption speak in a language the stone understood: confession, signature, and witness.

The captain who had sold his own city stumbled through his own undoing with trembling lips and a mouth full of coin-stink. The courier chain cracked. The “Markarth Drop” stopped being rumor and became ledger fact—routes, handoffs, bribes, and the kind of careful sabotage that only looks like chaos to people who aren’t paying attention. Igmund heard it. Fasendil heard it. The court heard it. Markarth’s rot didn’t vanish, but it began to purge like a wound finally cut open.

That should have been the end of it. It wasn’t.

Agran felt the shift first—an ugly pressure behind the eyes, a migraine with teeth. The world blinked wrong, and for a moment reality held its breath as a stranger entered the city gates in a shape that kept changing. Two silhouettes, one presence, like a story trying to decide which version to make true. The Dragonbreak didn’t roar like a storm; it whispered like a door that shouldn’t exist.

And the name that surfaced through that fracture was not “Crescent Witness,” not “handler,” not “courier.”

It was **Ondolemar**.

The Justiciar moved through Markarth with the serenity of a man who thinks stone belongs to him. His words were polished. His demand was simple: surrender what Agran had pulled into daylight—evidence, chain-of-custody, and the Daedric mace now riding at Agran’s hip like a hungry judge. He didn’t threaten like a bandit. He threatened like an empire that’s used to being obeyed.

Agran answered like a soldier who’d already stopped being afraid of titles.

Fasendil and Agran moved in tandem—one using Markarth’s connections, one using Markarth’s angles. Doors were locked. Clergy were enlisted. The Temple of Dibella became a quiet intake point, a stone throat that swallowed names off the ledger before Thalmor hands could reach them. In the stairwell, Agran turned charm into weaponry: bardic calm, velvet menace, and the kind of presence that makes even enemies hesitate long enough for plans to finish happening.

The Justiciar retreated—not defeated, not yet, but forced to move. Forced to improvise.

And improvisation is where the Reach lives.

Agran did not meet Ondolemar on the Justiciar’s terms. He met him on Reachland terms.

Through Cidhna Mine—through the stone belly where Madanach broods like an old curse—Agran bought help with something rarer than gold: shared hatred and a promise of blood paid forward. Kaie, Forsworn swiftblade and insider, took the bargain with a look that measured whether Agran was merely another outlander playing at local war. The Wolf spoke to her not as an Imperial officer, but as a Breton who knew the Reach’s history has never been kind to anyone who didn’t fight for it.

The plan was geometry. The killbox was punctuation.

Night fell, and the vampire in Agran stepped into its element like he’d been waiting all day to breathe. Scouts died quietly. A mage didn’t finish his spell. The Mace of Molag Bal tasted blood and answered with stolen strength—wounds knitting under armor, hunger made efficient. Forsworn shapes moved through brush and stone like old spirits, and the ridgeline itself became a trap door.

Ondolemar arrived with his “control” still on his tongue. He tried to speak. Agran tried something sharper than steel: ego-violence, the kind that strips the Thalmor of the only thing they worship without admitting it. The Justiciar’s mask cracked. His formation wavered. His escort looked at the darkness and realized it had teeth.

Then the Wolf closed the distance.

Ondolemar’s blood was not ordinary. It was *expensive*—ranked, curated, and tied to a machine of power that believes it owns the future. Agran took it anyway. He drank deep enough to make the night go silent around him. And when Ondolemar’s body finally failed, Agran didn’t just kill him.

He **sent him**.

The Mace ignited with a violet hunger, and the etched maw of Molag Bal became more than decoration. Ondolemar’s soul tore loose in a flash of cruel light—bound, swallowed, and dragged toward Coldharbour like a chain finding its anchor. It was not mercy. It was not justice. It was a statement made in Daedric iron:

*You don’t get to leave clean.*

The remaining Thalmor saw it. Even if they couldn’t name what they’d witnessed, they understood the moral: this wasn’t a battlefield anymore. It was a sermon.

Agran did not linger to gloat. He moved—fast, predatory, controlled. Another throat opened under fangs. Another pulse stopped. The Forsworn hit the rest like a collapsed cliff face, and the killbox did what it was built to do: end problems.

And somewhere behind Agran’s eyes, something else shifted—quiet, internal, inevitable. The night didn’t just empower him. It *recognized* him.

Not as a random cursed soldier. Not as a lucky monster.

As an investment finally maturing.
